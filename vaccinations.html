Vaccination & preventive healthcare

Vaccines that protect the trip — and the decades after it

Support for travellers, adults, families and individuals needing vaccination for personal, occupational or medical reasons. We coordinate the vaccines that are clinically indicated, confirm eligibility, and record everything clearly.

  • Hepatitis B vaccination
  • Influenza (seasonal) vaccination
  • Pneumococcal vaccination
  • Rabies vaccination and risk assessment
  • Additional vaccines by clinical indication
Why vaccination planning matters

Some vaccines are for one trip. Others last a lifetime.

Travel vaccines are often treated as a checkbox. We take a wider view: the same consultation looks at what protects you now, what protects you on future trips, and what protects the people around you.

  • Eligibility assessment. Age, previous vaccination, health conditions, pregnancy and medication all affect what is suitable.
  • Schedule planning. Some courses need multiple doses — we plan them around your departure date.
  • Record keeping. A clear vaccination record is issued so you can evidence immunisation at your destination or for work.
  • Post-vaccination advice. What to expect in the 48 hours afterwards, and when to seek medical review.

The process

How a vaccination visit works

Simple, unhurried, and properly documented.

Travel & health brief

Where you are going, how long for, what you will be doing, and any relevant medical conditions or past vaccination.

Eligibility review

A clinician confirms what is clinically appropriate, what is contraindicated, and whether a dose has already been given.

Consent & vaccination

Consent is taken and the vaccine is administered by a qualified professional, with the batch and site recorded.

Record & advice

You receive a vaccination record and practical aftercare guidance, including what to do if you feel unwell.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Which vaccines are available right now?

Availability changes with supply and is confirmed before booking. We currently coordinate HPV (Gardasil 9), hepatitis B, influenza, pneumococcal and rabies vaccination, and — depending on clinical indication and stock — tetanus, MMR, hepatitis A, typhoid and Japanese encephalitis. Contact us with the vaccine and date you need and we will confirm.

How far in advance should I get travel vaccinations?

Ideally four to six weeks before departure, because several vaccines require more than one dose and some need time to build protection. If your trip is sooner, tell us the date — we will prioritise what gives you the most benefit in the time available.

Can I be vaccinated if I am pregnant or breastfeeding?

Sometimes — live vaccines are generally avoided in pregnancy, while non-live vaccines may still be appropriate. Tell us at booking so a clinician can review your specific situation and advise accordingly. Some vaccines are not recommended in early pregnancy or while breastfeeding.

What if I have already received a dose of a vaccine?

Bring your record. Doses given elsewhere are usually counted towards the course where the product, schedule and documentation are clear. If you have no written record, we can advise on whether a repeat dose or a blood test is appropriate.

What should I expect after a vaccination?

Most people feel nothing beyond a mild sore arm. Common reactions include local pain, tiredness, headache or a mild fever lasting a day or two. We will give you written aftercare advice and explain which symptoms require medical review — including the rare signs of a severe allergic reaction.

Do you vaccinate children?

Yes, where the vaccine, the age range and the clinical indication are appropriate, and where a parent or guardian attends. Bring any existing vaccination records so the course can be reviewed accurately.

How we build a kit

Six questions define your travel kit

A kit that ignores where you are going, how you will travel, or what you are already taking is a guess. Ours starts with the specifics.

01

Where are you going?

Country and specific regions — a city stay and a rural high-altitude trip have completely different risk profiles.

02

How long, and how often?

Duration determines quantity. Frequent short trips may be better served by a standing supply arrangement than a one-off kit.

03

What will you be doing?

Treking, safari, rural volunteering, city business, visiting relatives, or a medical trip — each carries different exposure risks.

04

What are you already taking?

Current prescriptions, supplements, allergies and recent medical history shape both the contents and the quantity.

05

What can you get there?

Access to pharmacies, hospitals and specific medicines at your destination determines what must be carried and what can wait.

06

How do you travel?

Route, transit countries and mode of travel affect documentation requirements and what is allowed through customs.

Travel-day reality

What to actually carry in your hand luggage

Kits are useful, but the rules on what travels with you, in which bag, in what quantity, are what actually matter at the border.

  • Keep medicines in hand luggage, not checked baggage. Baggage is delayed, lost and occasionally searched.
  • Carry more than you think you need. Extra supply absorbs delays, cancellations and unexpected overnight stays.
  • Keep original packaging and labels. Repacked or unlabelled items attract attention and questions.
  • Carry the prescription and a clinician's letter. A single sheet naming each medicine, dose and purpose answers most questions.
  • Declare accurately. Never guess on a declaration form — an inaccurate declaration creates far more problems than a permitted medicine.
  • Check transit countries too. A medicine legal at your destination may need permission at a connection point.

Non-prescription items can be assembled for most destinations. Prescription items require a valid prescription and a lawful supply route.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How long before travel should I order a kit?

Two to four weeks is comfortable, particularly if prescription items, clinical review or documentation is involved. Shorter timelines are often possible for non-prescription travel-health items — contact us with your date and we will tell you honestly what is achievable.

Can you include prescription medicines in the kit?

Only where they have been appropriately prescribed, a valid prescription is available and a lawful supply route exists. Where we cannot supply an item, we will explain why and help you source it through your own prescriber.

Will the kit work for a long trip?

Yes — quantity is calculated from your dosing schedule and trip length rather than a fixed package size. For trips beyond three months we would normally recommend a standing arrangement with your prescriber instead of a single large supply.

What if I am travelling to several countries?

We build the kit around the strictest destination rather than the easiest. We will flag where the same medicine is treated differently and prepare the documentation for the country with the tightest requirements, including transit points.

Do you deliver kits outside Kathmandu?

Delivery and collection are subject to availability and location. Contact us with your city and we will confirm what is possible and what the alternatives are.

Pharmacy & prescription support

Keep your treatment running smoothly — at home, at work and abroad

Clinical pharmacy support for anyone who needs to continue prescribed medication. We review prescriptions, coordinate supply, advise on correct storage and handling, and plan ahead so you are not caught short.

What this service covers

  • Prescription verification and review
  • Medication supply coordination and labelling
  • Storage, handling and travel conditions
  • Interaction and adherence support
  • Continuity planning for long journeys
  • Coordination with your prescriber and hospital
Who this is for

Medication support for real life

Long-term medication is routine for millions of people — and it becomes complicated quickly when work schedules change, a trip is booked, a new medicine is added, or a family member needs help.

  • Patients on long-term medicines — refills, repeat prescription coordination and medication reviews.
  • People travelling frequently — maintaining an adequate supply and managing time-zone and storage effects.
  • Older adults and their carers — organisation of medication schedules, packaging and administration support.
  • People with several medicines — structured review and simplification discussions with your prescriber.
  • Patients after discharge — bridging supply while a hospital prescription is formalised at your local clinic.
Our services

What a Clinic Pharmacy prescription review covers

Every review is tailored to the individual. The list below shows the elements we most commonly address.

Prescription verification & review

We check the prescriber details, medicine name, strength, dosage, route, frequency and duration, and flag anything unclear for clarification with the prescribing clinician.

Medication supply coordination

Where a lawful supply route exists, we coordinate dispensing, labelling and packaging — in original packaging, with the pharmacy label retained where permitted.

Storage & handling advice

Guidance on refrigeration, temperature ranges, humidity, light exposure and shelf life during storage or transit — including cold-chain planning for travel.

Medication continuity planning

A practical schedule for trips and long journeys: what to take, when to take it across time zones, and how much supply to carry in case of delays.

Interaction & adherence support

A structured look at what you take, when you take it and why it is difficult — with findings referred back to your prescriber for clinical decisions.

Caregiver & family support

Simple, clear medication lists and schedules so that a family member or caregiver can support treatment confidently at home.

The process

How a medication review works

Five straightforward steps, most completed remotely or in a single clinic visit.

Submit your list

Share your prescriptions, medicines in hand, and the reason you need support — before travel, after discharge, or for routine review.

Clinical review

A qualified professional reviews the prescription details, duration, interactions and storage requirements.

Plan & advise

You receive a clear plan: what is available, what needs a prescriber's decision, and how to store and use each medicine.

Supply & documents

Where legally possible, medicines are dispensed and labelled, and any supporting letters or medication lists are prepared.

Follow-up

A check-in to confirm the plan is working, with referral back to your prescriber for anything clinical.

What we need from you

Bring these to your appointment

A short, practical checklist. Having these ready means your visit is faster and your plan is more complete.

  • All current prescriptions, or clear photographs of them
  • Every medicine you actually take, including non-prescription items and supplements
  • Your medical history, allergies and current diagnoses
  • Details of your hospital, specialist and regular pharmacy, where relevant
  • Your itinerary and travel dates if medication continuity is the reason for the visit
  • Any airline, employer, embassy or immigration requirements you have been given

Prescriptions are never issued here.

All prescriptions and treatment decisions remain the responsibility of authorised healthcare professionals. Clinic Pharmacy reviews, documents, coordinates supply and advises — it does not replace your prescriber.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an appointment for a prescription review?

For travellers and for anyone with several medicines, yes — we want to review the full list rather than individual items. Simple repeat-refill coordination can often be arranged by phone or email where a recent review is already on file. We will tell you which applies when you contact us.

Can you supply medicines that need a specialist prescription?

Supply depends on the medicine, the law and a lawful source. Where a supply route is available and lawful we coordinate it; where it is not, we explain why and help you arrange an alternative through your own prescriber. We never supply medicines without a valid prescription.

Will my medication be repacked for travel?

Generally medicines are supplied in their original packaging with the pharmacy label retained. Repacking can create problems at customs, with airlines and for controlled substances, so we avoid it unless there is a clinical reason and legal clearance.

How much medication should I take for a long trip?

Enough to cover the itinerary plus a reasonable buffer for delays — many travellers are caught out by missed connections. We calculate a quantity based on your dosing schedule and trip length, then check the destination country's import rules for those medicines.

Do you deliver to my home?

Delivery can be arranged in covered service areas for some patients and is subject to availability and clinical suitability. Contact us with your location and we will confirm what is possible.

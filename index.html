Vaccination & preventive healthcare

Protect the trip — and the years after it

Some vaccines protect for a single journey. Others — such as HPV and hepatitis B — protect for decades. We help you choose what is clinically indicated rather than what is simply available.

Gardasil 9 — HPV vaccination

Information and vaccination coordination for protection against nine types of human papillomavirus associated with cervical cancer, other HPV-related cancers and genital warts. Eligibility and dosing depend on age, previous vaccination and clinical guidance.

Hepatitis B vaccination

Preventive vaccination for eligible individuals seeking protection against hepatitis B, including travellers, healthcare workers and people with relevant exposure risks.

Influenza vaccination

Seasonal influenza prevention based on current recommendations, individual health considerations and travel timing.

Pneumococcal vaccination

Assessment and vaccination guidance for eligible individuals seeking protection against pneumococcal infections, particularly those with age-related or medical risk factors.

Rabies vaccination

Pre-travel rabies risk assessment and vaccination guidance for travellers visiting remote destinations or areas where timely access to rabies treatment may be limited. Urgent assessment after a possible rabies exposure is also important.

Customised international medicine kits

A travel kit built for your trip — not off a shelf

Each kit is assembled around your itinerary, trip duration, planned activities, medical history and access to healthcare at your destination.

  • Travel-health supplies selected for your specific itinerary and activities
  • Prescription medication planning, with quantities and suitable packaging
  • Destination-specific guidance on restricted or permit-controlled medicines
  • First-aid essentials, oral rehydration, motion sickness and insect-bite protection
  • Pre-travel review of interactions, storage requirements and time-zone effects
  • Medication declaration support for airline, customs and border authorities

Prescription medicines are included only where appropriately prescribed and legally supplied.

Non-prescription items can be assembled for most destinations. Prescription items require a valid prescription and a lawful supply route.

Common questions

Before you get in touch

Can you help me take a controlled medicine across a border?

We provide documentation and compliance guidance — specialist consultation coordination, prescription verification, customised medication travel letters and advice on destination-country restrictions, import permits and declaration procedures. We cannot guarantee that a medicine will be permitted across any border; the destination country and its authorities make that decision, and controlled medicines are handled in accordance with applicable Nepalese law.

How far in advance should I plan before travelling?

Ideally four to six weeks for first-time travel with vaccines, since some schedules require multiple doses. For repeat travellers on routine medicines, a few days is usually enough for prescription review, supply coordination and documentation. Last-minute requests are accepted where clinically and legally possible.

Do I need a doctor’s appointment for a medical certificate?

Yes. Certificates are issued only following the relevant assessment by an appropriately authorised healthcare professional. The scope of investigations and documentation depends on the requesting institution’s requirements, so please tell us which country, employer or institution the certificate is for.

Is home care available outside Kathmandu?

Home-care delivery is subject to clinical suitability, staffing, service availability and geographic coverage. Contact us with your location and we will confirm feasibility before any booking is made.

Which vaccines are currently available?

Availability changes with supply. We currently coordinate HPV (Gardasil 9), hepatitis B, influenza, pneumococcal and rabies vaccination, and — depending on clinical indications and stock — tetanus, MMR, hepatitis A, typhoid and Japanese encephalitis. Eligibility, schedules and administration are confirmed before booking.

Do you replace or review my existing prescriptions?

No. All prescriptions and treatment decisions remain the responsibility of authorised healthcare professionals. Our role is to review, document, coordinate supply and advise on travel-specific compliance so your treatment continues safely abroad.
